# Sources, assumptions, conflicts and gaps

Support Score data extract for the partner communication app.
Generated by `build.py`, checked by `validate.py`.

## Sources used

### 1. Volpi and Konig (2026)

* **Title:** Symptoms and distress across the menstrual cycle in a
  representative sample of Austrian and German people who menstruate: a
  cross sectional study
* **Journal:** Women's Health, Volume 22, 1 to 17
* **DOI:** 10.1177/17455057261462827
* **Licence:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY). Verified from the
  licence statement printed in the article. **Reuse allowed, including
  commercially, with attribution.**
* **Sample:** N = 336 menstruating participants from Germany and Austria,
  sampled to be representative on education, gender and age.
* **Used for:** `symptom_weights.csv` (all rows) and `phase_multipliers.csv`
  (endometriosis rows).

Two PDFs were supplied for this study. The file named `DHS__Menstrual_distress.pdf`
is the author preprint of the same paper, not a separate source. All values
were taken from the published version.

### 2. Armour, Sinclair, Chalmers and Smith (2019)

* **Title:** Self management strategies amongst Australian women with
  endometriosis: a national online survey
* **Journal:** BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 19:17
* **DOI:** 10.1186/s12906-019-2431-x
* **Licence:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY), with CC0 public
  domain dedication on the underlying data. Verified from the article.
  **Reuse allowed, including commercially, with attribution.**
* **Sample:** 484 usable responses from Australian women aged 18 to 45 with
  a self reported confirmed endometriosis diagnosis, recruited October to
  December 2017 through endometriosis advocacy groups on social media.
* **Used for:** `strategies.csv` (all rows).

## How each number was derived

### symptom_weights.csv

* `prevalence` = 1 minus the share who reported "symptom does not occur"
  (Volpi Table 3).
* `impact` = mean distress divided by 3, because distress was measured on a
  4 point scale from 0 (no distress) to 3 (severe distress).
* `weight` = prevalence times impact.

Volpi reports only the five most distressing symptoms per phase, so each
symptom carries the values from the phase where it appears. The phase used
is recorded in the `notes` column of every row.

### phase_multipliers.csv

Volpi reports **absolute mean distress per phase, not multipliers**:
menstrual 1.41, premenstrual 1.06, intermenstrual 0.98, all on the 0 to 3
scale. Multipliers were derived by dividing each phase mean by the
intermenstrual mean, the quietest phase, giving menstrual 1.439,
premenstrual 1.082, intermenstrual 1.0.

### strategies.csv

Usage share, mean pain relief on a 0 to 10 scale, and adverse event rate
come directly from Armour's usage list, Table 3 and Table 4. Ingestible and
pharmacological modalities (cannabis, hemp and CBD oil, dietary change,
herbal medicine, alcohol) were excluded to keep the project inside its non
medical scope.

`evidence_level` is assigned by subgroup size: `moderate` where 100 or more
respondents used the modality, `low` below that. Nothing is rated `high`.
Both sources are cross sectional self report surveys, which cannot support a
high rating on their own.

## Assumptions

Ten rows carry an `ASSUMPTION` flag.

| Where | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| `phase_multipliers.csv`, follicular | The study's intermenstrual window covers follicular and ovulatory days together and does not separate them. |
| `phase_multipliers.csv`, ovulatory | No separate ovulatory measurement exists. The intermenstrual value is reused. |
| `phase_multipliers.csv`, luteal | The study measured a **premenstrual** window, which is the late luteal phase only. Applying it across the whole luteal phase likely overstates days 16 to 22. |
| `phase_multipliers.csv`, all 4 PMOS rows | No source covers PMOS cycle variation. Set to the neutral 1.0. |
| `symptom_weights.csv`, 3 endometriosis rows | The paper reports subgroup distress means but no subgroup occurrence rate, so prevalence is carried over from the whole sample. The impact values are measured, not assumed. |

One further judgement, applied but not flagged per row: the endometriosis
phase multipliers use whole sample phase means. This is defensible rather
than assumed, because the paper explicitly tested for a phase by diagnosis
interaction and found none (p = .63). The endometriosis group sat higher on
pain, bleeding discomfort and impairment across phases, but the **shape** of
the cycle did not differ. That supports a model where the condition sets the
baseline and the phase supplies the multiplier. The caveat is that a non
significant interaction in a small subgroup may simply be underpowered.

## Conflicts

None. The two sources cover different questions (symptom distress across the
cycle, and self management strategy use) and do not overlap on any value, so
there was nothing to reconcile.

## Gaps

1. **No PMOS data at all.** Neither source covers polyendocrine metabolic
   ovarian syndrome. `symptom_weights.csv` has zero PMOS rows and
   `strategies.csv` has zero PMOS rows. The four PMOS rows in
   `phase_multipliers.csv` are neutral placeholders. Half the app's stated
   scope is currently unsupported by evidence.

2. **Symptom weights are general population, not endometriosis specific.**
   Nine of the twelve rows describe menstruating people in general. Only
   three rows are endometriosis specific, and they come from a post hoc,
   non preregistered sensitivity analysis. One of those three (abdominal
   pain in the menstrual phase) did not reach statistical significance
   (p = .057).

3. **Strategy evidence is narrow.** Every strategy row traces to one survey
   of 484 self selected Australian women recruited through advocacy groups,
   relying on recall. That population is more engaged and probably more
   severely affected than average. The relief scores are what those women
   reported, not trial results.

4. **Four phases were forced out of three.** The source measured menstrual,
   premenstrual and intermenstrual. The schema wants menstrual, follicular,
   ovulatory and luteal. Two of the four phases carry a reused value.

5. **Pain relief is not symptom impact.** Armour's 0 to 10 scale measures how
   much a strategy relieved pain. It does not measure how disruptive a
   symptom is. The two must not be mixed in the Support Score formula.
